How it works

  1. One king per ticker. Every ticker (the uppercase symbol, like CRWN) has one crowned token. The first token registered for a ticker is its king. The CRWN launch token was crowned the moment its pool opened, and its beneficiary is the launch payer's wallet.
  2. Every swap pays the king. Pools that use the CrownHook take a small cut of each swap (1% by default, never more than 5%), always in ETH. The cut goes to the current king's beneficiary in the same transaction. The hook never keeps the money. If the payment can't go through, the swap still happens. It goes ahead either without the cut or with the cut paid as a claim the beneficiary can redeem.
  3. Anyone can challenge. After a king has reigned for 7 days, anyone can put up a 0.01 ETH bond to challenge it with another token that has the same symbol. Only one challenge per ticker can run at a time.
  4. Stake to vote. For 72 hours anyone can stake ETH for the king or for the challenger.
  5. Resolution. After the 72 hours and another 24-hour timelock, anyone can finalize. The challenger wins only if it has more stake than the king and at least 0.05 ETH was staked in total. If the challenger wins, the registry crowns the challenger's token and every later swap pays the new king.
  6. Payouts. The winning side gets all of its stake back. The losing side gets 80% back. The other 20% is slashed: half goes to the winning side's stakers in proportion to their stake, and half goes to the CROWN treasury. Everyone claims their own share with Claim.
  7. No admin. The registry has no owner and no admin functions. Every outcome follows these rules.
